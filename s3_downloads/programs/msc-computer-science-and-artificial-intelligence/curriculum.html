MSc Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence

Programme Knowledge: Admissions and applicant counselling reference

1 Programme identity and positioning

 

The programme proposition

Computer Science for the AI era is the educational positioning territory. The programme develops the knowledge and judgement needed to design, build, secure, deploy and operate computing systems and intelligent applications. AI is both an area for advanced study and a capability that increasingly shapes practice across computing disciplines.

For working professionals, the conversation begins with the work they already do and the capabilities they want to strengthen. For fresh graduates, it begins with academic readiness, the common foundation and the professional work they want to learn. Working professionals lead the audience framing throughout counselling.

How AI connects with computing

  • Using AI to assist engineering work, such as generating code or tests, while checking correctness and security.
  • Embedding AI into an application or workflow, with appropriate data access, evaluation, monitoring and controls.
  • Developing or operating AI systems, including models, infrastructure and the surrounding software components.

These are explanatory examples of the programme philosophy, not promises of specific assignments.

3 Indian eligibility and academic readiness

 

The qualifying academic requirement is a three-year or four-year bachelor’s degree with Mathematics or Statistics as a subject. BITS Pilani determines whether professional experience qualifies as relevant. Experience concessions apply to the marks threshold; the remaining educational requirements continue to apply.

Applicants from different academic backgrounds

Eligibility is not restricted to Computer Science or engineering graduates. Prior CS study and programming experience are not separate mandatory admission conditions. The degree, result and subject evidence must meet the prescribed route and be verified.

Alternative without Mathematics or Statistics

The stated alternative is concurrent enrolment in a STEM postgraduate programme in science or engineering that includes Mathematics. Submit programme and subject details for assessment. Open resources can support preparation but do not replace formal eligibility.

Returning to formal study

Professional experience can provide practical context and problem-solving discipline. Focus, consistent effort and a regular routine help learners rebuild academic confidence after time away from study.

Early courses include Mathematical Foundations for Computing and Programming and Data Structures. They strengthen foundations for advanced study. Open resources and steady practice can help refresh familiar concepts.

4 International eligibility and admission assessment

Applicants from other countries

Applicants should hold a three-year or four-year STEM degree from an accredited university or one recognised by their country of residence, with Mathematics or Statistics as a subject. The stated threshold is an overall CGPA of at least 6.0 or overall marks of at least 60%.

The framework accepts an equivalence certificate from AIU, UGC or an equivalent statutory body of India. For applicants without Mathematics or Statistics in the qualifying degree, the stated alternative is concurrent enrolment in a STEM postgraduate programme in science or engineering that includes Mathematics.

Individual cases involving overseas qualifications, citizenship or residence, unfamiliar grade scales or unclear subject equivalence should be reviewed by Admissions. Confirm the applicable examination arrangements for applicants based outside India.

Admission approach

There is no entrance test. The programme welcomes working professionals seeking deeper expertise or a new career direction, as well as eligible fresh graduates seeking advanced computing capabilities.

Admission is based on the prescribed eligibility requirements and verification of academic qualifications, overall marks and Mathematics or Statistics coursework. BITS Pilani also evaluates the relevance of experience where a marks concession is requested.

Applicants should submit a complete application, provide the required evidence and respond promptly to clarification requests. The admission decision and any applicable conditions are communicated through official admission correspondence. Submission or a payment alone should not be described as an admission decision.

Evidence for a productive eligibility review

  • Degree title, duration, awarding institution and academic result as recorded in the official documents.
  • Transcript evidence of Mathematics or Statistics, or details of the stated alternative where applicable.
  • Professional experience information relevant to a requested marks concession.
  • Equivalence evidence and grade-conversion clarification where required by Admissions.

The application portal and Admissions determine the final document checklist. Do not quote a fixed decision turnaround or make an interview commitment without an official programme-specific instruction.

Reference: S1 Eligibility and Input Criteria; U1 confirmation of no entrance test. International experience concessions are not established in S1.

5 Learning delivery and academic support

Weekly learning cycle

Recorded content is released on a weekly schedule, generally in shorter video segments. Learners can access it at a convenient time during the weekly learning cycle and return to it later for revision. Live faculty interaction takes place on weekends. Additional practical sessions may be required depending on the course.

This format helps working professionals organise study around delivery deadlines, on-call duties and personal responsibilities. Eligible fresh graduates can use the same weekly structure to establish disciplined independent learning.

Academic rigour and milestones

The programme is flexible in format but rigorous in academic expectations. Learners must complete quizzes, assignments, practical work, projects and examinations according to the applicable academic requirements and schedule. Watching recorded material alone does not complete a course.

The final programme document describes synchronous interaction as typically about one hour per week but does not establish whether that figure is per course or for the programme as a whole. Counsellors should use the issued timetable and course handouts rather than quote a total weekly workload or live-hours commitment.

Support for questions and practical work

Learners can seek clarification through weekend live classes with faculty, discussion forums and teaching-assistant support. Discussion forums provide a way to raise questions between scheduled sessions and learn from peer discussions. Responses are addressed within reasonable timeframes; immediate replies should not be promised.

A useful support request explains the concept or task, the approach attempted and the precise difficulty. For coding work, relevant code and error messages can help faculty and teaching assistants give focused guidance. Raising questions early allows time to use that guidance before deadlines.

The Capstone includes academic supervision. Regular practice, active participation and timely use of the available support help learners develop confidence and maintain progress.

Study planning guidance

  • Protect recurring time for recorded learning, coding and problem-solving.
  • Plan assignments and project work around deadlines rather than leaving them to the live-session day.
  • Review work peaks and examination travel early.
  • Seek guidance promptly when progress is affected. Reduced pace and approved breaks remain subject to academic rules.

Reference: S1 Programme delivery and assessment and Experiential Learning; U1 weekly access, weekend classes, forums and teaching-assistant clarification.

6 Assessment and examinations

Continuous assessment includes quizzes, assignments and project work. Course handouts specify the evaluation components, their weightings and deadlines. Learners must follow the applicable submission and assessment instructions.

Trimester-end examinations are mandatory and take place offline at designated centres for learners residing in India. The programme document provides regular and makeup slot options. Learners should follow the published cohort timetable and eligibility rules for those arrangements.

Plan around the issued examination dates. The programme document describes weekend examinations; the general degree policy also refers to Friday, Saturday or Sunday slots. Use the actual schedule for leave and travel planning.

Use the currently communicated centre list. The programme document gives an approximate network size, while the public degree-policy page lists a different current count. A fixed centre count should therefore not be promised in applicant conversations.

Missed assessments and an uncleared course

A learner affected by an unexpected work or personal situation should contact the academic team promptly and follow the prescribed process. Extensions, reattempts and progression decisions depend on the academic rules. A work emergency does not itself establish an entitlement to an extension or extra attempt.

For an uncleared course, seek the academic team’s instructions on the available route, outstanding requirements and any effect on fees or progression. Programme-specific reattempt limits and minimum graduation grades should be confirmed from the applicable academic regulations.

Flexible pace and completion period

The nominal duration is six trimesters over two years. A more relaxed pace may be permitted through flexible schedules, including pause/resume and part-time study, within a maximum of 12 trimesters or four years. Such arrangements follow the Institute’s academic rules and approval process.

Reference: S1 Programme delivery and assessment and Nominal Duration; S4 Degree Programs. Centre counts, detailed grading and reattempt rules require the applicable cohort communications.

8 Graduation requirements and second year pathways

Standard degree structure

 

Formal credit requirements

  • At least four units of required Advanced Apex Projects.
  • A 12-unit Capstone Project.
  • At least 60 units from core, discipline elective and specialisation requirements, including permitted Advanced Apex Project credits.
  • At least 72 units from coursework, the Capstone and additional Advanced Apex Projects.
  • Up to six units of Advanced Apex Projects beyond the minimum four units may count towards the Discipline Elective requirement.
  • Total graduation requirement: four required Advanced Apex units plus 72 units from coursework, the Capstone and additional Apex work, totalling 76 units.

Second year pathway choices

 

Both pathways also include the required Advanced Apex work. Advanced Apex Project II carries two units. The source trimester pattern places it in Trimester 4, the Capstone in Trimester 5 and four electives in Trimester 6. Advanced Apex Project III is an optional two-unit course, subject to academic requirements.

A specialisation requires at least 18 units from its stipulated pool: four specified core courses and two electives. Students may choose no specialisation or one specialisation. Eligible courses from any specialisation core or elective pool may be taken as discipline electives when prerequisites are met.

Reference: S1 Graduation requirements, trimester-wise pattern, and Specialisation requirements and Curated Pathways. Project credit treatment must follow the academic rules.

14 Practical learning and programme outcomes

Course laboratories and assignments

Practice labs and lab-based assignments form part of almost every course. They connect conceptual study with implementation and require learners to evaluate results, explain decisions and validate AI-assisted outputs.

Required Advanced Apex Projects

The two required Apex Projects combine learning from several courses. Learners formulate a problem, select an approach, implement a solution and evaluate results. The project descriptions include system design, validation and presentation of technical work. Additional Apex work follows the academic requirements.

Capstone Project

The one-trimester Capstone carries 12 units. Projects are designed and vetted with industry partners around realistic requirements and constraints. Learners formulate the problem, design and implement a solution, test and evaluate it, and present a demonstrable outcome. Deployment is included where appropriate. Academic supervision and assessment maintain the postgraduate standard.

Programme learning outcomes

  • Apply foundational knowledge of mathematics, programming, algorithms, computer architecture, operating systems, networks and databases to analyse and solve computing problems, including those arising in AI-enabled systems.
  • Design and develop software systems using appropriate architectures, data structures, development practices, testing methods and security principles, integrating AI capabilities and development tools where appropriate.
  • Build and evaluate AI-enabled applications using machine learning and generative AI methods, with attention to data quality, performance, limitations and responsible use.
  • Design, deploy and operate computing solutions using suitable cloud, data, automation and monitoring technologies, including infrastructure for AI workloads and AI-assisted operations.
  • Investigate complex problems by formulating requirements, comparing technical alternatives, assessing the suitability of AI-based approaches, implementing solutions and evaluating results against stated criteria.
  • Apply advanced knowledge in a chosen specialisation, where selected, while understanding how AI intersects with its practices and how the specialisation connects to the wider computing system.
  • Communicate technical work effectively through documentation, demonstrations, reports and presentations, and collaborate responsibly in project and professional settings, exercising technical judgement and accountability when using AI tools and outputs.

Reference: S1 Program Learning Outcomes, Experiential Learning and project course descriptions.

18 Fees and published degree policies

Programme fee quotation

Use the approved CS and AI fee schedule and the individual offer for application, admission and tuition amounts and payment dates. Confirm the total payable under the learner’s proposed study plan. Financing, discounts and scholarships require current programme-specific confirmation. Do not substitute fee amounts from another programme.

Published degree policy

The public degree policy requires full trimester fees for each registered trimester, regardless of course count. Approved breaks attract a continuity fee of ₹10,000 per trimester of absence, payable on return. Breaks require approval and must be requested before the trimester begins. Confirm the applicable CS and AI terms before quoting costs.

For withdrawal within 30 days of the first live contact session of the first trimester, the policy provides a full trimester-fee refund. Application and admission fees are non-refundable. Submit requests through Admissions and retain acknowledgement.

Cloud and GPU usage

Advanced courses or projects may need cloud or GPU resources beyond shared or open-source environments. The learner bears additional usage costs; these are outside the programme fee. Costs vary by resources and activity. Requirements and estimated cost guidance are provided before the relevant module.

Admission compliance

The public policy requires Indian students to submit a DEB ID within 30 days of batch commencement. It also sets a 30-day equivalence-certificate deadline for international students. Follow the applicable admission instructions.
